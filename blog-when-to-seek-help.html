When should you consider talking to a mental health professional?

There is a common misconception that therapy is only for people experiencing a mental illness or going through a major crisis. But in reality, you don't need to be struggling severely to benefit from speaking with a mental health professional.

Therapy provides you with a safe and supportive space to understand your own thoughts, emotions, behaviours, relationships, and the challenges that you may be experiencing.

Let's see when might therapy be helpful:

Everyday we all go through various difficulties, stressors, and challenges in our lives. While some of these are a natural part of everyday life, there are times when our thoughts, emotions, and even our bodies may signal that we are struggling more than usual. Recognising these signs can be an important first step and sometimes, seeking professional support can help us understand what we are experiencing and navigate it more effectively:

1. You feel overwhelmed more often than usual

Stress is a normal part of life, but when feelings of stress, anxiety, sadness, or emotional exhaustion begin to feel persistent or difficult to manage, talking to a psychologist can help you understand what may be contributing to these feelings.

2. You find yourself overthinking constantly

Repeatedly analysing situations, worrying about the future, or getting stuck in "what if" thoughts can become exhausting. Therapy can help you recognise unhelpful thinking patterns and develop healthier ways of responding to them.

3. Your relationships are becoming difficult

If you are struggling with communication, setting boundaries, people-pleasing, conflict, trust, or repeated relationship patterns, therapy can help you understand these difficulties and work towards healthier ways of relating to yourself and others.

4. You are struggling with self-esteem

Constantly criticising yourself, comparing yourself with others, feeling inadequate, or relying heavily on external validation can affect both your emotional wellbeing and everyday decisions. Therapy can help you develop a more balanced and compassionate relationship with yourself.

5. You are going through a significant life change

Moving, starting or ending a relationship, changing careers, academic pressure, grief, or other major transitions can bring uncertainty and emotional stress. You don't have to navigate these changes alone.

6. Your usual coping strategies aren't helping anymore

Everyone develops ways of coping with difficult emotions. However, strategies such as avoidance, excessive scrolling, withdrawing from others, or constantly distracting yourself may provide temporary relief without addressing the underlying issue. A psychologist can help you explore what you are experiencing and develop coping strategies that are healthier and more sustainable.

7. Your daily functioning is being affected

Changes in sleep, appetite, concentration, motivation, work or academic performance, or relationships can sometimes indicate that your emotional wellbeing needs attention. When these changes persist or interfere with everyday life, professional support may be beneficial.

You don't have to wait for a crisis

One of the most important things to remember is that therapy isn't only for when things become unbearable.

You can seek therapy because you want to understand yourself better, improve your relationships, manage stress more effectively, work through a difficult experience, or simply feel more equipped to navigate life's challenges.

Seeking support isn't a sign that you are unable to cope. Sometimes, it is a way of choosing to understand yourself and take better care of your mental wellbeing.

If something has been weighing on you for a while, you don't have to wait until it gets worse before reaching out for support.
